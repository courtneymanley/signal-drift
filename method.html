The Capture Project

Method

How the captures on the log are annotated, and why they don't all look the same.

A screenshot is raw material. What goes on this site is something built out of it. Every capture here carries two things, kept apart: a record and an annotation.

Record and annotation

The record is the paperwork: source, format, byline, when it was published and when I captured it. It sits under every capture, folded away. A record could be written by someone who's never seen the rest of the log.

An annotation couldn't. The rule I'm holding myself to is simple: annotate what the image can't say. If a note just retypes the headline, it gets cut. The screenshot already shows that better than I can. What's left is timing, what's missing, what changed from the capture before it, and what I still can't tell.

One move per status

Where a capture sits under the Capture Rule decides how it's annotated. The form is part of the argument, so it's consistent. You can tell what a capture is doing on this site before you read a word.

dMarginalia

For every capture in a chain. Numbered pins mark the exact places where a version changed, and the notes sit in the margin. Between captures, the connector names the point of change.

cFacing pages

For chains that are only contested. The disagreeing versions sit side by side under one shared point of divergence, and you connect them yourself.

hSequence

Used once. Three captures in the order I found them, and nothing else. The order is the annotation.

fGrid, one caption

For isolated captures that make a pattern together but don't connect to one underlying fact. The pile gets one paragraph.

eCrop and enlarge

For single captures where the claim lives in one line. The whole capture sits small and the line is blown up. Cropping is a claim, so the full screenshot always stays in view next to it.

bWords on the image

For single isolated captures. A few words inside the frame, placed on purpose.

gRedaction

Used for the guardrails, not for effect. Anything private or unrelated is blacked out in the image file itself, not drawn over it, so saving the image can't bring it back. Each record says what was covered and why.

aCaption below

The default, kept for what's out of scope. If a capture looks ordinary here, that's the point. It's outside the argument.

What isn't here

One capture is in my folder but not on this site. It's a personal post shared with a friends-only audience, so it breaks the rule that everything here has to already be public. It stays out of the log.
