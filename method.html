The Capture Project

Method

How I annotate the captures on the log, and why they don't all look the same.

A screenshot is raw material. What ends up on this site is something I build out of it. Every capture here has two parts, and I keep them separate: a record and an annotation.

Record and annotation

The record is the paperwork: source, format, byline, when it was published and when I captured it. It's folded away under every capture. Someone who's never seen the rest of the log could write a record.

They couldn't write the annotation. My rule for myself is simple: annotate what the image can't say. If a note just repeats the headline, I cut it, because the screenshot already shows that better than I can. What's left is timing, what's missing, what changed from the capture before it, and what I still can't tell.

One move per status

Where a capture falls under the Capture Rule decides how I annotate it. The form is part of the argument, so I keep it consistent. You should be able to tell what a capture is doing here before you read a word.

dMarginalia

For every capture in a chain. Numbered pins mark the exact spots where a version changed, and the notes sit in the margin. Between captures, a connector names the point of change.

cFacing pages

For chains that are only contested. The versions that disagree sit side by side under the one point where they split, and I leave it to you to connect them.

hSequence

I use this once. Two captures in the order I found them, and nothing else. The order is the annotation.

fGrid, one caption

For isolated captures that make a pattern together but don't share one underlying fact. The whole group gets one paragraph.

eCrop and enlarge

For single captures where the claim is all in one line. The full capture sits small and the line gets blown up. Cropping is a claim in itself, so the full screenshot always stays in view next to it.

bWords on the image

For single isolated captures. A few words inside the frame, placed on purpose.

gRedaction

This is for the guardrails, not for effect. Anything private or unrelated is blacked out in the image file itself, not drawn on top, so saving the image can't bring it back. Each record says what I covered and why.

aCaption below

The default, and I save it for what's out of scope. If a capture looks plain here, that's on purpose. It's outside the argument.

Checked against the source

When I check a capture against a primary source, like an agency's own notice, I link the source under the capture and mark it Checked. If I haven't been able to confirm something, it stays off the page.

Arrival

Every chain has one more stop after national, official and local, and it's me. Each chain capture shows how long it took to reach me, counted from the publish or update time on the capture to the moment I took the screenshot. If a capture only shows a date, or a stamp like “1d,” that's what it says instead.

Starting in late October, every new capture also gets one line I write when I take it, before I go looking for other versions: what made me stop. Earlier captures don't have one, and their records say so. I'd rather leave that gap than write a first impression after the fact.

What isn't here

Three captures are in my folder but not on this site. One is a personal post shared with a friends-only audience, so it breaks my rule that everything here has to already be public. The other two are from the first week. I filed them under an earlier version of this project, before the Capture Rule changed, so they stay out of the log.
