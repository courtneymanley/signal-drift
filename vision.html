The Capture Project

Vision Statement

Signal Drift is a semester-long capture project for CMM 502: Organizational Writing and Design Across Media. This page is the argument behind the log — what it's actually trying to show, and why the site is built the way it is.

A health story rarely stays one story. It changes as it moves — from the original incident, to national coverage, to an official notice, to a hyperlocal post — picking up and dropping context at every stop. Sometimes it splits into versions that outright disagree. Signal Drift is a running, public record of that movement: pairs and chains of real, already-public health communication that share one underlying fact, filed alongside the point where the messaging visibly changed.

What I expected to find

This started as a project about attention span. The working idea was simple: health stories sound urgent when they hit, and go quiet on slow-moving threats, or once the story stops being news. That thesis is what got the project moving — but it turned out to be only half the story, and maybe not the important half.

What I found instead

Early on, three captures taken days apart described the same underlying event: a national post about roughly 300 people exposed to rabies at a petting zoo, an official health alert on the same exposure, and a local community post about a related case nearby. Rather than one story fading over time, it became three different stories, depending on how far each version had traveled from the original event.

A second pattern showed up around the same time in measles outbreak coverage: several outlets reported confirmed deaths, while a health official publicly called that same coverage “fearmongering.” Not a story fading — two versions of it, actively disagreeing about what the facts even meant.

That changed what this project is actually about. It's no longer centered on urgency fading over time — that can still be part of it. The main thread now is how health stories are reframed as they move, and where, exactly, the messaging changes shape.

The capture rule

Nothing on this site is filed as a single, standalone item. A capture is only added once it connects to at least one other version of the same underlying fact — either because that version diverges some other way, or because it disagrees outright. Two items that are only topically similar don't count: a general story about tick season isn't the same as three outlets covering one specific outbreak. The connecting thread has to be a specific, identifiable event or data point.

Every chain on the log is filed as one of the following:

Revised facts were simplified, dropped, or updated as the story moved — shape changed through translation, not disagreement. Contested the versions disagree about which facts are true in the first place. Revised + Contested both are happening in the same chain — which is often the most honest tag for a story. Isolated logged and kept on file, but no linked version has turned up yet.

What this should do for you

The goal isn't to convince you the media lied. It's narrower, and harder to shake:

“I only saw one version of this, and I have no idea what it looked like at the scale above or below me, or from someone who wanted a different story to be true.”

If a story on this log changes how you read the next health headline you come across — enough to wonder what version you're actually looking at — that's the project working.

How the log is built

Each entry on the log is a chain: two or more captures, in the order they were found. A revised chain reads top to bottom, with a note at each step explaining the point of change — what shifted from the capture before it. A contested chain reads as a fork instead: one shared point of divergence, then the disagreeing versions side by side, each with its own note on how it diverges. Anything that hasn't linked to another version yet is kept, visibly separate, in an isolated section at the bottom of the log — logged, not discarded, in case a link turns up later.

The commitment behind it: one health-related capture a day, working toward 100 by the end of the semester. Chain-building — the searching for a second or third linked version — happens alongside that daily habit and doesn't count against the total.

Guardrails

Everything captured here was already public: a news story, an advertisement, a public-facing post, an official notice. Nothing private is in scope — no direct messages, texts, or screenshots from a group chat, mine or anyone else's. If a public post quotes or reposts something private, that part is cropped out or skipped entirely. When a contested pair involves a public figure's statement, that person is treated as a public communicator speaking in their public capacity, not as a personal target.
