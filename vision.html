The Capture Project

Vision Statement

Signal Drift is my capture project for CMM 502: Organizational Writing and Design Across Media. This page is the thinking behind the log: what I'm trying to show, and why I built the site the way I did.

A health story rarely stays one story. It moves from the original incident to national coverage, to an official notice, to a post from somewhere close to home, and it picks up and drops context at every stop. Sometimes it splits into versions that disagree. This site is my running record of that movement. Every chain on it is real, already-public health communication that shares one underlying fact, or one illness whose coverage keeps rising until it reaches my area. Each one is filed with the point where the messaging changed.

What I expected to find

I started this project thinking it would be about attention span. My working idea was that health stories sound urgent when they hit, then go quiet once the story stops being news, especially when the threat is a slow one. That idea is what got the project moving. But it turned out to be only half the story, and maybe not the important half.

What I found instead

Early on, I captured three posts a few days apart that were all tracking the same rising story. The first was a national post about roughly 300 people exposed to rabies at a petting zoo in North Carolina. The second was a nationwide CDC alert about rising rabies exposures. The third was a local post about a bat that tested positive here in the Hudson Valley. They weren't one event. They were one illness getting more coverage, first nationally, then officially, then close to home. I expected to watch a story fade. Instead, the coverage kept rising, and it changed shape at every stop.

Around the same time, I noticed a second pattern in the measles coverage. Several outlets reported confirmed deaths in Pennsylvania, while the health secretary said politicians and media were “trying to scare” Americans about measles. That wasn't a story fading either. It was two versions of the same story, disagreeing about what the coverage even meant.

That changed what this project is about. Fading urgency can still be part of it. But the main thread now is how health stories get reframed as they move, and where exactly the messaging changes.

The capture rule

A capture only becomes part of a chain once it connects to at least one other version, and there are two ways that can happen. The first is the same underlying fact: a specific event or data point, told again somewhere else. The second is rising coverage: a specific illness, disease or piece of commentary that starts getting more attention and begins showing up locally. Two items that are only about the same topic don't count. A general story about tick season isn't a chain. Three stories that show rabies coverage rising until it reaches my own region are.

Every chain on the log gets one of these tags:

Revised the facts were simplified, dropped or updated as the story moved. Nobody's disagreeing, but the shape changed along the way. Contested the versions disagree about which facts are true in the first place. Revised + Contested both are happening in the same chain. When a story fits both, I tag it as both. Isolated logged and kept on file, but I haven't found a linked version yet.

What this should do for you

I'm not trying to convince you the media lied to you. What I want is smaller than that, and I think it sticks longer. I want you to leave thinking:

“I only saw one version of this, and I have no idea what it looked like at the scale above or below me, or from someone who wanted a different story to be true.”

If something on this log changes how you read the next health headline you see, even just enough to make you wonder which version you're looking at, then the project did its job.

How the log is built

Each entry on the log is a chain: two or more captures, in the order I found them. A revised chain reads top to bottom, with a note at each step about what changed from the capture before it. A contested chain reads as a fork instead. There's one shared point where the versions split, then the versions side by side, each with its own note. Every chain opens with an overview so you can see the whole thing at once, and you can pull up any two versions next to each other to compare them.

Anything that hasn't linked to another version yet sits in its own section at the bottom of the log. I keep those instead of throwing them out, in case a link turns up later.

My commitment is one health-related capture a day, working toward 100 by the end of the semester. Searching for a second or third version to build a chain happens alongside that habit, and those searches don't count toward the 100.

Guardrails

Everything on this site was already public when I captured it: a news story, an ad, a public-facing post, an official notice. Nothing private is in scope. That means no direct messages, no texts and no screenshots from group chats, mine or anyone else's. If a public post quotes or reposts something private, I crop that part out or skip the post. When a contested pair involves a public figure, I'm treating that person as someone speaking in a public role, not as a personal target.
